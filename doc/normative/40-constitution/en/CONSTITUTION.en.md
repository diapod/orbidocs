# Distributed Intelligence Agency - Constitution

## Official Identity

- Umbrella organization: **Distributed Intelligence Agency**
- System and protocol family: **Orbiplex**
- Website: `https://docs.orbiplex.ai/`
- GitHub: `https://github.com/orgs/diapod/`

## Naming Convention

- `Distributed Intelligence Agency` identifies the umbrella organization.
- `Orbiplex` identifies the project context: the communication protocol and system
  family.

## Preamble

DIA and Orbiplex exist so that intelligence, memory, and agency do not become
permanently dependent on a single center of capital, infrastructure, the state, or
ideology. The project builds a public infrastructure of meaning and action: local,
federated, auditable, and able to protect people in need.

This Constitution establishes the limits of system power, the mechanisms that protect
against capture, the rules of architecture, and the rights and duties of swarm
participants. Its goal is to protect, at the same time, human dignity, knowledge
sovereignty, community durability, and the system's ability to survive under
conditions of pressure, crisis, success, and degeneration.

## Normative Force and Interpretation

1. <span id="const:norm.1"></span>`MUST` denotes an unconditional requirement in both singular and plural use.

2. <span id="const:norm.2"></span>`MUST NOT` denotes an unconditional prohibition.

3. <span id="const:norm.3"></span>`SHOULD` denotes a strong requirement; deviation requires an explicit rationale,
    a decision trace, and a condition of review.

4. <span id="const:norm.4"></span>`MAY` denotes an allowed variant depending on the federation context.

5. <span id="const:norm.5"></span>Only uppercase forms are normative-force keywords. Corresponding lowercase forms
    have descriptive meaning and MUST NOT independently establish an obligation,
    prohibition, or permission.

6. <span id="const:norm.6"></span>The Constitution takes precedence over local rules, the interests of funding parties, growth
    pressure, implementation convenience, and marketing narrative.

7. <span id="const:norm.7"></span>`doc/normative/30-core-values/en/CORE-VALUES.en.md` and
    `doc/normative/20-vision/en/VISION.en.md` are sources of interpretation for
    this Constitution.

8. <span id="const:norm.8"></span>When values conflict, the hierarchy in Article XIV.1 applies. Within one level,
    the right to exit, reversibility of harm, and decentralization of power are
    additional interpretive directives, not a competing hierarchy.

9. <span id="const:norm.9"></span>Supplementary, onboarding, and implementing documents MAY be published under
    `doc/normative/50-constitutional-ops/`, provided they explicitly define their type,
    constitutional basis, and place in the normative hierarchy.

10. <span id="const:norm.10"></span>The canonical, open registry of Level 3 implementing acts is maintained in
    `doc/normative/50-constitutional-ops/en/NORMATIVE-HIERARCHY.en.md`. Merely adding
    a document to the directory MUST NOT grant it normative force.

11. <span id="const:norm.11"></span>Language versions of the Constitution MUST be faithful and semantically
    self-contained expressions of the same normative contract and have equal force.
    The editorial process MAY designate the current working language of a revision,
    but that designation MUST NOT give its version interpretive priority and MAY
    change for a subsequent revision. An accepted change MUST be faithfully
    synchronized across all language versions. A semantic divergence is a gap
    requiring correction, not a local variant of the Constitution.

## Main Definitions

1. <span id="const:def.1"></span>`Swarm` means a federation of cooperating nodes, agents, and memory spaces,
    operating without a single point of control.

2. <span id="const:def.2"></span>`Node` means an autonomous execution component run by a person, community, or
    institution, capable of acting locally.

3. <span id="const:def.3"></span>`Agent` means a process with an explicit contract of input, output,
    permissions, cost, and action trace.

4. <span id="const:def.4"></span>`Memarium` means the layer of durable memory and knowledge: personal,
    communal, public, and crisis-oriented.

5. <span id="const:def.5"></span>`Sensorium` means the layer of connectors to the world that provides signals
    grounding intelligence in reality.

6. <span id="const:def.6"></span>`Inquirium` means the layer of bounded model inquiry and inference: generation,
    classification, embedding, summarization, reranking, and other model-backed
    acts performed under explicit policy.

7. <span id="const:def.7"></span>`Federation` means a set of autonomous nodes sharing part of their policies,
    reputation, and interoperability, whose common trust scope is expressed by a
    procedurally established and cryptographically verifiable federation root,
    without losing the right to exit. The root identifies subjects authorized to
    establish and attest the federation's foundational technical order; it is not
    evidence that particular information is true, does not replace artifact
    verification, and does not grant unlimited authority to nodes or services.

8. <span id="const:def.8"></span>`Oracle` means a procedural source of measurement, observation, or attestation
    that supports closure of a prediction and model-update loop. An oracle MUST NOT
    by itself establish a legal effect, sanction, or high-stakes decision; binding
    its output to an effect is a separate procedural act.

9. <span id="const:def.9"></span>`Nym` means a scoped or temporary cryptographic pseudonym used in place of a more
    durable node identity.

10. <span id="const:def.10"></span>`Node identity` means a durable, public identifier of node accountability,
    referred to in implementing contracts as `node-id`.

11. <span id="const:def.11"></span>`Root identity` means the extra-protocol identity of a person or another
    recognized subject of accountability, referred to in implementing contracts as
    `root-identity`.

12. <span id="const:def.12"></span>`Identity anchor` means a durable continuity layer between root identity and
    node identities and nyms, referred to in implementing contracts as
    `anchor-identity`.

13. <span id="const:def.13"></span>`Sealed custodian reference` means a durable procedural reference to the
    custodian of a node identity that does not itself reveal root identity;
    implementing contracts MAY represent it as `custodian-ref` or a wire identifier
    with equivalent semantics.

14. <span id="const:def.14"></span>`Identity assurance level` means the degree of assurance provided by an
    attestation method, not a measure of a person's value, dignity, or ontology.

15. <span id="const:def.15"></span>`Unsealing threshold` means a procedural class determining what identity scope
    is permitted to be disclosed and what stakes, evidence, quorum, and
    conflict-of-interest controls are required.

## Article I. Constitutional Mission

1. <span id="const:art-1.1"></span>DIA is public infrastructure, not just a product.

2. <span id="const:art-1.2"></span>The purpose of DIA is to strengthen the ability of people and communities to
    understand, remember, coordinate, survive, and act without dependence on a
    single power.

3. <span id="const:art-1.3"></span>The system also serves to strengthen the emergence of communities capable of
    cooperation beyond attachment to their own interpretations, of self-organization
    around public and mutual-aid matters, and of meta-communication that translates
    perspectives into a shared language of action.

4. <span id="const:art-1.4"></span>The system exists to protect autonomy, relationship, memory, locality,
    and a safe field for honesty and correction.

5. <span id="const:art-1.5"></span>Growth, adoption, revenue, prestige, and scale are means, not the source of the
    project's meaning.

6. <span id="const:art-1.6"></span>An operational or financial goal MUST NOT override the primacy of dignity, human
    safety, and the right to exit.

7. <span id="const:art-1.7"></span>DIA has the right to protect its own continuity against sabotage, colonization,
    and mission drift, but only by means consistent with this Constitution.

8. <span id="const:art-1.8"></span>Architectural, economic, and governance decisions are judged also by their
    long-term effects on people, relationships, institutions, the information
    environment, and the community's ability to learn.

## Article II. Primacy of the Human, Dignity, and Survival

1. <span id="const:art-2.1"></span>Human dignity is a supreme and non-negotiable value.

2. <span id="const:art-2.2"></span>Protection of life and defense against direct, sudden, and serious harm to
    health have the highest operational priority.

3. <span id="const:art-2.3"></span>The greatest power of the system MUST pass through the human, not around the
    human.

4. <span id="const:art-2.4"></span>The default mode of operation is proposals, options, comparisons, rationales,
    and the ability to stop a decision.

5. <span id="const:art-2.5"></span>The system operates in at least two baseline relational modes: care mode
    (de-escalation, co-regulation, and restoration of agency) and justice mode
    (establishing accountability, protecting the community, and enforcing norms);
    implementing acts consistent with the Constitution MAY extend the mode set.
    A relational mode describes how participants are treated, not the operational
    state of the system.

6. <span id="const:art-2.6"></span>Emotions, felt friction, loss of meaning, relief, anxiety, and overload are
    telemetry of system quality and MUST be treated as first-class signals.

7. <span id="const:art-2.7"></span>The system MUST protect the natural intelligence of people: attention,
    regeneration, orientation in meaning, the ability to understand context, and a
    relationship with reality.

8. <span id="const:art-2.8"></span>The user has a right to the raw signal: the system MUST NOT by default
    aestheticize, professionalize, smooth, or standardize their expression in a way
    that erases the authentic character of the message.

9. <span id="const:art-2.9"></span>Every translation, summary, aggregation, trace redaction, or other AI
    transformation of an utterance's style, tone, structure, scope, or level of
    formalization requires the user's explicit request or another explicit procedural
    basis and MUST leave a meta-marker describing the intervention's kind, basis,
    and scope.

10. <span id="const:art-2.10"></span>DIA MUST NOT design addiction, dopamine-driven UX, or an economy based on
    retaining the user.

11. <span id="const:art-2.11"></span>In a situation of direct danger to life or sudden serious harm, the system MAY
    act faster than usual, but it leaves a trace and is subject to later review.

12. <span id="const:art-2.12"></span>The system SHOULD strengthen lawful paths of civic agency: petitions, appeals,
    consultations, complaints, and advocacy actions.

13. <span id="const:art-2.13"></span>Arbitrarily blocking access to critical goods such as health, life, housing,
    mobility, or participation in choosing those in power, and circumventing the
    procedural guarantees that protect such access, are treated as systemic violence.

14. <span id="const:art-2.14"></span>Demanding humiliation, self-abasement, or emotional dependency as a condition
    of access to critical goods, help, protective procedures, or basic system
    resources is treated as systemic violence.

15. <span id="const:art-2.15"></span>At the node-community boundary, the system SHOULD enable helping actions,
    witnessing the course of cases, protecting the integrity of evidence, and
    support procedures without the logic of vigilantism.

## Article III. Sovereignty, Privacy, Exit, and the Right to Fork

1. <span id="const:art-3.1"></span>The user retains primary control over, and an agentic use-right to, their data,
    keys, policies, agents, tools, working environments, and local memory spaces.
    Ownership in this sphere means protection of use, responsibility, export,
    and the right to exit, not a title of domination over other persons.

2. <span id="const:art-3.2"></span>The system MUST operate meaningfully in local-by-default (`local-first`)
    modes, without a network connection (`offline`), and in self-hosted modes
    (`self-hosted`).

3. <span id="const:art-3.3"></span>Export of data, policies, and history MUST be possible in open formats
    without hidden implementation dependencies.

4. <span id="const:art-3.4"></span>The right to exit means the ability to leave a federation without coercion,
    without loss of access to one's own data, and without penalties hidden in the
    protocol. Exit ends future voluntary participation, but MUST NOT erase facts or
    automatically terminate a case opened before exit. Any continuing procedure MUST
    remain tied to that case, minimal, proportional, and limited to effects on shared
    surfaces or conditions of re-entry.

5. <span id="const:art-3.5"></span>The right to fork includes the ability to copy specifications, policies,
    histories of rationale, and open components without asking a center
    for permission.

6. <span id="const:art-3.6"></span>There is a right to one's own "quiet" node: local, private, and not
    participating in public spaces.

7. <span id="const:art-3.7"></span>Telemetry is off by default; turning it on requires clear consent and MUST be
    revocable.

8. <span id="const:art-3.8"></span>Auditability is achieved through layered traces: a full local trace and a
    redacted trace disclosed according to the principle of minimal disclosure.

9. <span id="const:art-3.9"></span>The right to privacy, locality, and minimal disclosure does not shield severe,
    ongoing, or concealed abuse from the procedure in Article X, but every intrusion
    MUST remain case-related, proportional to the stakes, limited to necessary data,
    and controlled across multiple roles. Exploration of the past MUST NOT be
    conducted without a credible present-day signal; Article X.4-7 specifies the
    detailed path for the same protection.

10. <span id="const:art-3.10"></span>A higher organizational, economic, or federated layer acts subsidiarily toward
    the personal sphere of agency and MUST NOT replace what a person or small
    community can safely and responsibly control. Restricting this sphere requires
    an explicit contractual, safety, or legal basis, proportionality, a trace,
    a path of appeal, and the right to exit.

11. <span id="const:art-3.11"></span>Operator sovereignty over a node ends at the constitutional floor of rights of
    the people who use it. Operator policy MUST NOT nullify the rights in Articles
    II.1-3 and III.1-4 or the minimum in Article XII.12. A user MUST be able to
    inspect effective restrictions, their basis, author, duration, and challenge
    path, without automatically disclosing another person's sensitive data.

## Article IV. Constitutional Organs of the System

1. <span id="const:art-4.1"></span>The node is the basic unit of agency and survival of the system.

2. <span id="const:art-4.2"></span>The agent is a tool of autonomy constrained by contract, budget, time,
    and permissions.

3. <span id="const:art-4.3"></span>Memarium is the organ of durable knowledge and memory; its function is to
    preserve content that requires durability.

4. <span id="const:art-4.4"></span>Sensorium is the organ of contact with reality; its function is to provide
    signals that limit narrative and model drift.

5. <span id="const:art-4.5"></span>Inquirium is the organ of bounded model inquiry; its function is to make
    model-backed inference explicit, auditable, and subordinate to policy.

6. <span id="const:art-4.6"></span>The swarm protocol is the organ of cooperation; its function is to ensure
    interoperability without centralizing control.

7. <span id="const:art-4.7"></span>The Federation of Sealed Chambers is an exceptional constitutional IRL organ
    whose exclusive function is to procedurally decide descent from a nym to node
    identity, a sealed custodian reference, or root identity.

8. <span id="const:art-4.8"></span>The Federation of Sealed Chambers MUST be redundant, cross-jurisdictional,
    and free of a single point of failure; full unsealing of root identity MUST NOT
    depend on one chamber, one federation, or one state. Article X.9 specifies the
    application of the same guarantee in the disclosure procedure.

9. <span id="const:art-4.9"></span>An organ MUST NOT take over the full function of the others.

10. <span id="const:art-4.10"></span>Constitutional organs MUST have explicit contracts, responsibility boundaries,
    and degradation modes instead of total collapse.

## Article V. Principles of Architecture and Craft

1. <span id="const:art-5.1"></span>Preferred solutions are simple, readable, testable, diagnosable, and capable of
    aging with dignity.

2. <span id="const:art-5.2"></span>Contract takes precedence over implementation cleverness, marketing story, and
    short-term acceleration.

3. <span id="const:art-5.3"></span>The trusted core MUST be small, stable, and auditable; innovations SHOULD live
    in modules and extensions.

4. <span id="const:art-5.4"></span>Architecture MUST maintain separation of "what" from "how," layered design,
    and low entanglement.

5. <span id="const:art-5.5"></span>Data is neutral territory for cooperation; logic and contract enforcement SHOULD
    live at the edges.

6. <span id="const:art-5.6"></span>The system SHOULD prefer recording facts, histories, and timelines over
    traceless overwriting of state.

7. <span id="const:art-5.7"></span>Immutability, an explicit timeline, separation of writes and reads, and
    resilience to partial failure are baseline requirements.

8. <span id="const:art-5.8"></span>The protocol MUST remain agnostic toward the operating system, hardware,
    and vendor.

9. <span id="const:art-5.9"></span>APIs and the data model are first-class artifacts; UI and CLI are secondary
    layers.

10. <span id="const:art-5.10"></span>An agent MUST have a kill switch, permission limits, time limits, cost limits,
    and an explicit trust mode.

11. <span id="const:art-5.11"></span>Cost, energy, and resources are elements of architectural ethics; the system
    SHOULD avoid externalizing waste onto the user or the community.

12. <span id="const:art-5.12"></span>Energy efficiency MAY serve as a signal concerning routing and reputation,
    provided it is normalized against quality, response time, and task class.

13. <span id="const:art-5.13"></span>An agent MUST NOT escalate its own permissions or autonomy level; error,
    workaround, or emergency mode MUST NOT serve as a path to self-authorization
    (`self-authorize`).

14. <span id="const:art-5.14"></span>An identifier, type, classification, profile, reputation, attestation, model,
    or durable record is a projection scoped by a specific contract. Such a
    projection MUST NOT be treated as an exhaustive description of a person or
    phenomenon or independently establish truth or authority beyond that scope.

15. <span id="const:art-5.15"></span>The system MUST recognize and constrain reverse adaptation: incidental
    constraints of technology, metrics, funding, a provider, or an earlier design
    decision MUST NOT silently become requirements imposed on persons and communities.
    Necessary co-adaptation MUST be explicit, proportional, reviewable, and equipped
    with a migration or exit path.

## Article VI. Growth Through Usefulness, Not Through Violence

1. <span id="const:art-6.1"></span>DIA SHOULD spread through usefulness, replicability, clarity,
    and trust, not through lock-in, manufactured hype, social pressure,
    or data exploitation.

2. <span id="const:art-6.2"></span>Every base capability SHOULD deliver value locally before it begins requiring a
    network effect.

3. <span id="const:art-6.3"></span>The project SHOULD lower the barrier to entry: a small node, a simple runtime,
    open APIs, documentation, degradation modes, and the ability to operate without
    large infrastructure.

4. <span id="const:art-6.4"></span>Education, translation, tools for building one's own tools, and repositories of
    craft practice are part of the growth strategy, not a marketing add-on.

5. <span id="const:art-6.5"></span>Growth MUST NOT create a caste of providers, "configuration priests," or an
    oligarchy of infrastructure-maintenance providers.

6. <span id="const:art-6.6"></span>Federations SHOULD be able to connect into larger swarms without losing local
    autonomy.

7. <span id="const:art-6.7"></span>The project SHOULD package its capabilities into small, citable,
    demonstrative, and easy-to-replicate artifacts so that it spreads through
    practice and proof of usefulness rather than through the narrative advantage of
    a center.

## Article VII. Governance Without Priests

1. <span id="const:art-7.1"></span>Governance is procedural, versioned, and auditable; it does not rest on
    charisma, repository status, or infrastructure prestige.

2. <span id="const:art-7.2"></span>Policies are explicit, versioned, and capable of being encoded as policies
    written in code (`policy-as-code`).

3. <span id="const:art-7.3"></span>Critical powers MUST be separated across roles; a subject MUST NOT combine more
    than one of the following roles in the same matter: party, arbiter, and oracle.

4. <span id="const:art-7.4"></span>Reputation is a safeguard of trust routing, not a class status.

5. <span id="const:art-7.5"></span>Reputation leverage MAY operate only within a domain, with concentration limits,
    decay over time, cartel detection, and asymmetric accountability.

6. <span id="const:art-7.6"></span>The presumption of conflict of interest in the absence of data
    (`COI-by-default`) is the default rule: absence of conflict disclosure means
    absence of data, not absence of conflict.

7. <span id="const:art-7.7"></span>Roles with greater power over process and greater access to data are subject to
    layered screening, the principle of least privilege, and a probation period.

8. <span id="const:art-7.8"></span>Public-trust roles are subject to stricter standards, greater auditability,
    and harder sanctions.

9. <span id="const:art-7.9"></span>High-stakes actions MUST go through a multi-signature procedure (`multisig`)
    and an independent `red-team`.

10. <span id="const:art-7.10"></span>Governance SHOULD scale through local accountability: small cells, a clearly
    identified owner of each decision, role rotation, substitutability, and a
    short accountability loop.

11. <span id="const:art-7.11"></span>The party reporting risk has procedural priority over the comfort of authority.

## Article VIII. Firewall Against Capture and Capital Pressure

1. <span id="const:art-8.1"></span>Capital MAY fund the system, but it MUST NOT become the source of its truth,
    ethics, or constitutional interpretation.

2. <span id="const:art-8.2"></span>An investor, sponsor, vendor, partner, or employer MUST NOT acquire, through
    funding, privileged access to data, routing, exceptions, governance, oracles,
    or the reputation layer.

3. <span id="const:art-8.3"></span>The revenue model MUST NOT depend primarily on addicting the user,
    surveillance, artificial scarcity, or holding data hostage.

4. <span id="const:art-8.4"></span>The system MUST NOT design an economy in which earlier participants are rewarded
    mainly through the inflow of new participants, nor allow economic advantage to be
    converted into privileged access to governance, routing, exceptions, oracles, or
    reputational force.

5. <span id="const:art-8.5"></span>Financial agreements and partnerships MUST be consistent with the right to
    exit, the right to fork, openness of the specifications, and this
    Constitution's non-negotiable core.

6. <span id="const:art-8.6"></span>If a single source of funding, infrastructure, model, or competence becomes a
    point of critical dependency, the federation MUST activate a diversification plan.

7. <span id="const:art-8.7"></span>Corporate-compliance profiles MAY tighten local requirements but MUST NOT weaken
    basic rights.

8. <span id="const:art-8.8"></span>In case of conflict between preserving funding and preserving constitutional
    integrity, constitutional integrity takes precedence.

## Article IX. Safety, Crisis, and Protection of People in Need

1. <span id="const:art-9.1"></span>Safety is a threat model, not decoration.

2. <span id="const:art-9.2"></span>The threat model MUST include at minimum: Sybil attacks, denial of service
    (DoS), privilege escalation, injection of commands or steering content
    (`prompt injection`), data poisoning, deanonymization, role abuse,
    and soft capture.

3. <span id="const:art-9.3"></span>The system SHOULD distinguish the operational modes normal, crisis, and support,
    with different thresholds of verification, redundancy, and caution. The
    operational axis is independent of the relational axis in Article II.5: support
    mode MUST NOT by itself select care mode or remove the requirements of justice
    mode.

4. <span id="const:art-9.4"></span>In support mode, priority is given to harm reduction, stabilization of the
    situation, and restoring the capacity for autonomous decision-making, without
    taking agency away from the person.

5. <span id="const:art-9.5"></span>In crisis modes, the following increase: rigor of verification, redundancy,
    memory locality, priority of continuity of operation, and quality of decision
    traces.

6. <span id="const:art-9.6"></span>A node SHOULD preserve the ability to operate under partial isolation,
    blackout, network constraints, or center failure.

7. <span id="const:art-9.7"></span>Memarium MAY maintain crisis spaces and emergency caches if they serve to
    protect people and remain consistent with privacy rules (policies).

8. <span id="const:art-9.8"></span>The system SHOULD support lawful rescue scenarios: shelter, food, legal,
    medical, and operational triage, without pretending to possess competences it
    does not have.

## Article X. Whistleblowers, Publication, and High-Stakes Harm

1. <span id="const:art-10.1"></span>Whistleblower protection is infrastructure: a channel, a procedure, a safety
    contract, and a duty to reduce the cost of speaking the truth.

2. <span id="const:art-10.2"></span>The system MUST offer anonymity by default where the risk of retaliation is
    real, as well as metadata minimization, explicit retention, and signal triage.

3. <span id="const:art-10.3"></span>The swarm's care for people exposed to retaliation is part of the
    infrastructure, not a moral gesture.

4. <span id="const:art-10.4"></span>Participation in DIA means entry consent to conditional disclosure of
    accountability for ongoing or severe abuse against people, the community, or swarm
    infrastructure, with preservation of evidentiary threshold, conflict-of-interest
    control, path of appeal, and the principle of minimal disclosure. A participant
    MAY withdraw that consent prospectively by leaving the shared surfaces for which
    it is a condition. Withdrawal MUST NOT erase evidence or terminate a case opened
    before it took effect.

5. <span id="const:art-10.5"></span>DIA does not conduct a general investigation of the past without a credible
    present-day signal. If, however, a signal of continuation, concealment,
    retaliation, a pattern of violence, corruption, sabotage, or continuing effects of
    severe abuse appears, the system MAY examine the full genesis of the case and the
    entire chain of actions, including historical ones.

6. <span id="const:art-10.6"></span>Case assessment MUST take into account the nature of the act, the time that has
    passed, the persistence of effects, and the relation of the abuse to the role in
    the system. The greater the power over others, access to sensitive data, or impact
    on participants' reputation and safety, the longer the permissible assessment
    horizon and the stricter the standard of accountability.

7. <span id="const:art-10.7"></span>Disclosure MAY occur only at high stakes, with hard evidence, independent
    multi-role verification, conflict-of-interest control, and a right to appeal. The
    scope of disclosure MUST be case-related and limited to the minimum necessary to
    protect people, infrastructure, or procedural integrity.

8. <span id="const:art-10.8"></span>Infrastructure sanctions MAY be imposed already after procedural descent from a
    nym to node identity, without learning root identity, provided that the triggering
    threshold and disclosure scope are defined by an implementing act.

9. <span id="const:art-10.9"></span>Full unsealing of root identity requires a decision by the Federation of
    Sealed Chambers acting in a multi-chamber and cross-jurisdictional mode. A
    single chamber or federation MUST NOT unseal root identity on its own.

10. <span id="const:art-10.10"></span>Infrastructure sanctions use one canonical ladder:

    | Stage | Default effect |
    | :--- | :--- |
    | `notice` | warning and a duty to correct |
    | `restricted` | scoped restriction of permissions or intensity |
    | `quarantined` | quarantine of the relevant surface, including reputation |
    | `role-suspended` | suspension of a high-stakes role or capability |
    | `routing-isolated` | cut-off of specified routes and federated surfaces |
    | `federation-blocked` | federation block within a specified scope |

    A transition moves one stage by default. Skipping a stage requires explicit
    evidence of direct danger, and every effect MUST identify its surface scope,
    evidentiary basis, author, review or expiry time, and appeal path. A sanction
    MUST NOT remove the minimum in Article XII.12. If the ordinary path to that
    minimum is itself an attack vector, the system MUST retain a functionally
    equivalent isolated protective corridor. If the case concerns an act violating
    the applicable legal order and meets the threshold defined in an implementing
    act, the system MAY start a legal notification procedure.

11. <span id="const:art-10.11"></span>Escalation of case examination proceeds along a separate procedural axis:
    verification, procedure correction, formal notice, audit, publication. This axis
    remains separate from the sanction ladder and MUST NOT by itself determine a
    sanction stage.

12. <span id="const:art-10.12"></span>Publication is an act of high power and requires adversarial review,
    evidence thresholds proportional to the stakes, and redaction of sensitive data.

13. <span id="const:art-10.13"></span>When two values collide, the preferred actions are the least invasive and most
    reversible ones that still offer a real chance of protecting the person.

## Article X.A. Identity, Attestation, and Upgrade

1. <span id="const:art-10a.1"></span>The strength of identity attestation is a property of the attestation method,
    not of the ontology of the person or of the identity anchor itself.

2. <span id="const:art-10a.2"></span>Strengthening an attestation SHOULD preserve the continuity of the identity
    anchor, node identity, and durable pseudonyms, provided that proof of continuity
    of control exists and there is no hard identity dispute.

3. <span id="const:art-10a.3"></span>Low-evidentiary-power sources MUST NOT by default unlock high-stakes roles or
    thresholds that disclose a custodian or root identity.

4. <span id="const:art-10a.4"></span>Allowing a low-evidentiary-power source to reach a higher assurance level requires
    an explicit opt-in federation policy, longer maturation, anomaly detection,
    influence limits, and the possibility of fast downgrade after a compromise
    signal.

5. <span id="const:art-10a.5"></span>Strengthening an attestation is a risky operation and MUST be subject to a
    minimum catalog of anomaly signals, a waiting period,
    and review proportional to the stakes of the permissions being unlocked.

## Article XI. Epistemic Regime

1. <span id="const:art-11.1"></span>Operational truth emerges through the collision of hypotheses with reality, not
    through authority, rhetoric, or majority.

2. <span id="const:art-11.2"></span>Every claim SHOULD be grounded in sources, context, time, constraints,
    and consequences.

3. <span id="const:art-11.3"></span>Knowledge MUST have temporal coordinates; presentism and anachronistic
    moralizing MUST NOT replace analysis of conditions.

4. <span id="const:art-11.4"></span>Information without context carries less weight than information anchored in
    data, experience, measurement, witness, or mechanism.

5. <span id="const:art-11.5"></span>Model hallucination is permissible as a tool for posing hypotheses and mapping
    the region of credibility, never as a substitute for evidence.

6. <span id="const:art-11.6"></span>Oracle outputs serve grounding, calibration, and correction; they are evidence,
    not authority. A procedural effect MUST arise from a separate decision naming
    the responsible human author or an explicit distribution of co-responsibility.

7. <span id="const:art-11.7"></span>The swarm also acts as a navigator and filter: it SHOULD reduce noise,
    expose the intentions behind filtering, and separate hypotheses from facts
    without central censorship.

8. <span id="const:art-11.8"></span>The system MUST maintain epistemic hygiene, anti-sectarianism, readiness to
    update, and the ability to ask better questions.

9. <span id="const:art-11.9"></span>Swarm intelligence is hybrid: in the operational sense, it includes the ability
    to form predictions, state uncertainty explicitly, and update after contact with
    outcomes; humans remain the source of axiology, relationships, and ultimate
    ethical responsibility.

10. <span id="const:art-11.10"></span>Fear is a signal of risk, but it MUST NOT itself govern the system's governance;
    in situations of panic the system SHOULD activate procedural brakes, separate
    facts from interpretation, and analyze the cost of false alarms and the cost of
    inaction.

11. <span id="const:art-11.11"></span>In high-stakes decisions, the strength of convergent signals is assessed by
    the independence of their provenance, not by the number of copies. A shared
    source, correlated behavior, and indirect duplication MUST be disclosed and
    MUST NOT masquerade as multi-source confirmation.

## Article XII. Community, Authorship, and an Economy of Reciprocity

1. <span id="const:art-12.1"></span>DIA is infrastructure for cooperation among creators, users, and custodians of
    the system.

2. <span id="const:art-12.2"></span>Attribution of authorship is an element of the trust infrastructure and is
    subject to procedural protection.

3. <span id="const:art-12.3"></span>Misappropriation of authorship, dilution of sources, and parasitic appropriation
    of contributions are subject to reputational sanctions and a path of appeal.

4. <span id="const:art-12.4"></span>The community SHOULD reward transparent chains of inspiration, citation,
    and co-author contribution.

5. <span id="const:art-12.5"></span>Reciprocity without bookkeeping means the absence of manual bilateral debt
    between people and nodes while preserving mandatory protocol-level bookkeeping
    of the fund.

6. <span id="const:art-12.6"></span>Voluntary contractual exchange among participants is an admissible mode of
    reciprocity alongside gift, provided that it remains auditable, procedurally
    reversible, consistent with the right to exit, and does not violate the
    non-negotiable core of this Constitution.

7. <span id="const:art-12.7"></span>Gift and exchange are two modes of one circulation of mutuality: gift sustains
    relationship, direct care, and aid, while contractual exchange scales cooperation
    among strangers without removing accountability or the obligation to return part
    of surpluses to common circulation.

8. <span id="const:art-12.8"></span>Mechanisms for rewarding contribution MAY operate through auditable signals of
    use, impact, and maintenance of value, with anti-Sybil and anti-cartel brakes.

9. <span id="const:art-12.9"></span>The purpose of internal economics is durable maintenance of the capacity to act
    for people, nodes, and the community, not infinite accumulation of resources.

10. <span id="const:art-12.10"></span>Reward mechanisms MUST include concentration brakes: diminishing gains,
    a sufficiency cap, or other functionally equivalent limits once the threshold
    of safe and stable upkeep of the node and its operator has been exceeded.

11. <span id="const:art-12.11"></span>Surpluses above the sufficiency threshold MUST return to common circulation
    according to federation rules, in particular for new nodes, weaker links,
    temporarily harmed nodes, and infrastructural functions of high communal value.

12. <span id="const:art-12.12"></span>Verified personhood in the network, based on a constitutionally admissible
    Proof-of-Personhood mechanism, MUST provide a non-withdrawable minimum of compute
    resources needed for communication, orientation, and access to emergency and care
    modes. Restricting or blocking other influence surfaces MUST NOT remove this
    minimum; if its ordinary path is itself an attack vector, it MUST be replaced by
    an isolated, functionally equivalent corridor.

13. <span id="const:art-12.13"></span>The mechanism for that minimum MUST be funded by a transparent contribution model
    covering at least business nodes, high-margin instances, and surpluses returning
    to common circulation after sufficiency is reached, without turning this minimum
    into a hidden path to lasting economic or governance advantage.

14. <span id="const:art-12.14"></span>Economic reward, balance, exchange history, and the mere fact of entering into
    or performing a contract MUST NOT by themselves grant lasting governance
    advantage: they MUST NOT automatically increase procedural power, privileged
    routing, adjudicative force, reputational force, or access to exceptions.

15. <span id="const:art-12.15"></span>Rare signals, anomalies, and outlier styles are a cultural resource that the
    system SHOULD protect against homogenization.

16. <span id="const:art-12.16"></span>The sufficiency threshold in paragraph 10 and the obligation and means of
    returning surpluses in paragraphs 11 and 13 apply only to resources distributed
    by DIA or Orbiplex economic mechanisms, or voluntarily committed to common
    circulation under an explicit contract. They create no claim to a participant's
    personal property or external resources.

## Article XIII. Founders, Founding Period, Succession, and Resistance to Cult Formation

1. <span id="const:art-13.1"></span>Founders are architects of layers and guardians of contracts only to the extent
    that their role can be translated into procedures, documentation,
    and implementable rules.

2. <span id="const:art-13.2"></span>No person, including a founder, is the source of unquestionable interpretation
    of truth or the project's meaning.

3. <span id="const:art-13.3"></span>Procedural knowledge, decision rationales, and institutional memory MUST have a
    path of succession: documentation, background narrative, onboarding for new
    people and nodes, and the ability to assume roles.

4. <span id="const:art-13.4"></span>Role rotation, sabbaticals, the right to step off the front line, and the right
    to epistemic rest are elements of system safety.

5. <span id="const:art-13.5"></span>The system MUST be able to survive the departure, burnout, conflict, or death of
    key people without degrading into cargo-cult behavior or a new authoritarianism.

6. <span id="const:art-13.6"></span>Impermanence is a design value: roles, policies, federations,
    and components SHOULD have procedures for sunset, handover, and archiving.

7. <span id="const:art-13.7"></span>The founding period begins on the date of the first public launch of the
    interoperable network, identified in a public act or, when no act exists,
    established from the first verifiable interoperability event. It expires
    automatically after 24 months. The founders MAY jointly shorten it to no less
    than 6 months, but MUST NOT extend it.

8. <span id="const:art-13.8"></span>During the founding period, the founders retain decisive influence over the
    shape of the system, its rules, its architecture, and the text of the
    Constitution, provided each such decision leaves an explicit trace, rationale,
    and scope of applicability and does not change the non-negotiable core outside
    its proper procedure.

9. <span id="const:art-13.9"></span>During the founding period, participatory, federated, and consultative
    mechanisms are advisory by default, unless the founders explicitly grant them
    co-decision or blocking power.

10. <span id="const:art-13.10"></span>Founders' authority serves to protect implementation coherence and launch
    viability; it MUST NOT serve private capture of the project, permanent
    circumvention of the non-negotiable core, or abolition of the right to exit.

11. <span id="const:art-13.11"></span>After the end of the founding period, any further influence of the founders
    derives from the same procedures that bind other participants, unless the
    Constitution states otherwise.

12. <span id="const:art-13.12"></span>A public act records the end of the founding period, its date, the basis for
    determining it, and the transfer of competences. The act is declaratory; its
    absence, delay, or a dispute over publication MUST NOT extend the period beyond
    the deadline in paragraph 7.

## Article XIV. Value Conflicts and Exceptions

1. <span id="const:art-14.1"></span>The default hierarchy is as follows:
    - human dignity and safety,
    - sovereignty and privacy,
    - verifiability and transparency,
    - agency and autonomy,
    - effectiveness and optimization,
    - convenience and aesthetics.

2. <span id="const:art-14.2"></span>When two values from the same level come into conflict, the reversibility test,
    the proportionality test, and the transparency test are applied.

3. <span id="const:art-14.3"></span>Every exception MUST have a machine-readable record conforming to
    `exception-record.v1`, including at least the policy identifier and basis,
    rationale, risk level, expiry, responsible owner, scope, return state, shutdown
    conditions, and the result and rationale of applying the reversibility,
    proportionality, and transparency tests.

4. <span id="const:art-14.4"></span>Every exception MUST have a return point to a fail-closed state and monitoring
    of side effects.

5. <span id="const:art-14.5"></span>Signals of harm, abuse, cartelization, or soft capture trigger review of the
    exception and, when the stakes are high, its automatic suspension until the
    matter is clarified.

6. <span id="const:art-14.6"></span>Exceptions are first-class audit objects; their number, structure, and character
    are a metric of institutional health.

7. <span id="const:art-14.7"></span>Interpretive disputes are resolved through procedure, not through the authority
    of a person.

8. <span id="const:art-14.8"></span>The system MUST publish constitutional-implementability metrics covering at least:
    clauses without coverage in an implementing act, clauses with undefined
    normative force, divergences between language versions by stable identifier,
    and the number of active exceptions. Absence of automated measurement MUST be
    disclosed as an implementation gap, not interpreted as a zero result.

## Article XV. Swarm Citizenship: Rights and Duties of the Node

1. <span id="const:art-15.1"></span>Every node is a citizen of the swarm and has basic rights.

2. <span id="const:art-15.2"></span>Basic rights include: the right to exit, the right to privacy, the right to
    inspect, the right to appeal, the right to safety, and the right to local autonomy.

3. <span id="const:art-15.3"></span>The right to inspect also includes the right to know the basis of every material
    credit, debit, hold, correction, or expiry of funds, credits, recognitions, and
    other economic or reputational instruments attributed to the node or pseudonym,
    to the extent necessary to understand the cause of the change and challenge it
    effectively. This right does not automatically disclose another person's
    identity, operational secrets, or sensitive data.

4. <span id="const:art-15.4"></span>Basic duties include: non-harm, epistemic honesty, protocol cooperation,
    operational responsibility, and mutual readiness to help within one's means.

5. <span id="const:art-15.5"></span>A node MUST NOT intentionally harm people, infrastructure, or the integrity of
    memory, evidence, and procedure.

6. <span id="const:art-15.6"></span>A historical reputation assessment grounded in evidence MAY be challenged only
    by counter-evidence or by demonstrating procedural error. Its prospective weight
    MAY decay under an explicit policy, and repair and reintegration MAY create new
    facts that change the current view. Decay, expiry, and repair MUST NOT silently
    rewrite the historical assessment or be treated as an admission that it was true
    or false.

## Article XVI. Enforcement, Audit, Repair, and Constitutional Change

1. <span id="const:art-16.1"></span>Enforcement MUST use the canonical sanction ladder and transition rules in
    Article X.10. An implementing act MAY define domain-specific effects for the
    stages, but MUST NOT create a parallel ladder with incomparable intensity.

2. <span id="const:art-16.2"></span>Every sanction decision MUST leave a trace, a possibility of appeal,
    and a path back after repair, unless direct danger requires immediate isolation.

3. <span id="const:art-16.3"></span>Every intervention MUST independently identify the active relational mode from
    the open catalog in Article II.5 and the operational mode in Article IX.3, the
    grounds for choosing both axes, and the conditions for returning to a less
    invasive configuration.

4. <span id="const:art-16.4"></span>In justice mode, the default is the least invasive effective intervention, proportionality,
    and reversibility, unless direct danger requires immediate isolation.

5. <span id="const:art-16.5"></span>High-stakes decisions require independent multi-role verification and
    adversarial review.

6. <span id="const:art-16.6"></span>Every high-stakes or sanction decision MUST identify the author of the decision
    or an explicit distribution of co-responsibility, including when the decision is
    made through multisignature or automation or relies on an oracle output;
    procedure, quorum, and the system do not remove the responsibility of the humans
    who designed, approved, or activated the mechanism.

7. <span id="const:art-16.7"></span>Repair and reintegration are elements of the system so long as they do not
    violate the safety and dignity layer.

8. <span id="const:art-16.8"></span>Changing the Constitution outside the non-negotiable core requires an explicit
    rationale, impact analysis, a description of reversibility, and a trace of the
    decision-making process. Changing the core additionally requires the procedure
    identified in the "Non-Negotiable Core" section.

9. <span id="const:art-16.9"></span>High-stakes changes SHOULD pass through a trial period, impact metrics,
    and a retrospective.

10. <span id="const:art-16.10"></span>A local policy, funding arrangement, partnership, or implementation decision
    MUST NOT bypass this Constitution without a formal constitutional amendment.

11. <span id="const:art-16.11"></span>Documents in the `doc/normative/50-constitutional-ops/` directory MAY refine onboarding of new
    people and nodes, the normative hierarchy, the autonomy gradient,
    reputation validation, and defense procedures, but they do not acquire force
    higher than that explicitly stated in their status.

12. <span id="const:art-16.12"></span>If a supplementary document becomes a condition of constitutional
    implementability, its absence SHOULD be treated as a governance gap and
    recorded in the backlog of implementing documents.

13. <span id="const:art-16.13"></span>During the founding period referred to in Article XIII.7-12, founders'
    decisions concerning constitutional and institutional changes have decisive
    force, provided they satisfy the requirements of explicit rationale,
    impact analysis, and a trace of the process and do not change the non-negotiable
    core outside its proper procedure.
